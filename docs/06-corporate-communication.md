# Corporate communication

This is the shared visual and editorial system for proposals, board reports, business reviews, partner decks, policy documents, and operational PDFs.

## Presentation principles

- One message per slide.
- Use a dark forest-green title or section slide for navigation, not every slide.
- Keep normal content on an off-white or white surface.
- Use orange for one decision, metric, or CTA—not as decoration. `palette.orange-bright` (`#FF6A00`) is the brand orange for large display text, shapes, and highlights on dark green fields; use `accent` (`#C2410C`) for orange text or buttons on light surfaces.
- Prefer a short headline plus evidence over a paragraph wall.
- Put source, period, and owner on data slides.

## Slide architecture

| Slide | Purpose | Recommended layout |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Establish topic and owner | Dark green field, white logo, short title |
| Executive summary | Make the decision visible | 3 key points or metric cards |
| Section divider | Reset attention | Green field, section label, one sentence |
| Evidence | Explain what changed | Chart/table with one highlighted finding |
| Process | Explain how the system works | Numbered flow with green progression |
| Risk and action | Make next steps actionable | Risk, impact, owner, due date |
| Closing | Confirm decision or contact | Clear next action and TEMBUS signature |

## Presentation sizing

- Default format: 16:9 widescreen.
- Keep a 7% safe margin around the canvas.
- Use at least 18pt body text and 24pt for table text when the deck is presented live.
- Never place critical text in the bottom 5% where platform chrome or subtitles may overlap.

## Report architecture

Recommended order:

1. Cover and document metadata.
2. Executive summary.
3. Scope, period, and definitions.
4. Findings and evidence.
5. Risks, decisions, and recommendations.
6. Appendix, methodology, and change log.

Reports should include page numbers, document version, confidentiality classification when applicable, and a source note for material data.

## Tables and charts

- Use green for structure and selected series.
- Use orange only to call attention to the one value or condition needing action.
- Use red only for confirmed risk/failure, not low performance by default.
- Label values directly when possible.
- Never use color alone to distinguish series or status.
- Include an accessible text summary for every chart.
- Series colors come from the `dataviz` tokens; see [Data visualization](09-data-visualization.md).

## Voice

Write as a clear, accountable operator:

- state the fact;
- state the impact;
- state the decision or next action;
- name the owner and timing when known.

Avoid unsupported certainty, inflated claims, and internal jargon without a definition.
